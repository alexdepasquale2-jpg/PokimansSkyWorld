# Outreach

Every message here asks for **twenty minutes of advice about a workflow**. None
of them mention a product, because none of them can — at Gate 1 there isn't one,
and pretending otherwise converts a discovery interview into a sales call that
produces flattery instead of data.

## What not to say

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| "I'm building software for fire protection contractors" | They switch into buyer mode and start being polite |
| "Would you use a tool that…" | Hypothetical willingness is uncorrelated with real behaviour |
| "Quick question" as a subject line | Reads as a sales template; it is one |
| Anything with a demo link or landing page | Same |
| "I'll only take 5 minutes" | You need 25. Underselling the ask makes people cut you off at 5 |

If asked directly what you're building: *"Honestly, I don't know yet — I'm
trying to understand how the reporting side actually works before I decide
whether there's anything worth building. That's why I'm asking rather than
pitching."* It's disarming because it's true.

## Track A — Association and warm intro

Highest yield, slowest to open. Start on day one.

**To a chapter executive director:**

> Subject: Research on ITM reporting workflows — request for introductions
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm doing research into how fire protection contractors actually handle NFPA
> 25 and 72 inspection reporting — specifically the writeup and filing side,
> not the field work.
>
> I'm looking to interview about a dozen contractors for twenty minutes each.
> Not selling anything; I'm trying to understand the workflow before deciding
> whether there's a problem worth working on.
>
> Would you be open to a short call, or to pointing me at two or three members
> who deal with the reporting side and might be willing to talk?
>
> [Name] · [phone] · [physical address]

Chapter EDs are professionally in the business of connecting members to useful
things. A referral from one converts far better than any cold email.

**At a chapter meeting or trade show**, in person:

> "Can I ask you something about your ITM work? I'm trying to understand how
> the reporting side actually goes — who writes the report, when it gets done.
> Takes two minutes."

Then run the short-form version of the script and ask for a proper call.

## Track B — Cold outbound

Personalised, low volume, from a real person. Roughly 5–15% reply; about half of
repliers schedule.

**Email 1 — day 0**

> Subject: How does your team handle NFPA 25 writeups?
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm researching how fire protection contractors handle inspection reporting —
> the writeup and AHJ filing side specifically.
>
> [One specific, true observation: "I saw you're hiring an ITM technician for
> the [city] branch" / "your site says you cover [n] counties"]
>
> I'm not selling anything — I'm interviewing about a dozen contractors to
> understand how the process actually works. Twenty minutes, whenever suits.
> Happy to share what I learn across the interviews if that's useful.
>
> Would you be open to it?
>
> [Name] · [phone] · [physical address] · [unsubscribe]

**Email 2 — day 4**, reply in thread:

> Hi [Name] — following up on this. The specific thing I'm trying to understand
> is how much time goes into the writeup after the tech leaves the site, and who
> ends up doing it. If that's not your area, is there someone at [Firm] closer
> to it?

The "is there someone else" line does real work: it converts a non-answer into a
routed introduction, and it surfaces the owner-versus-ops-manager split the
sampling quota cares about.

**Email 3 — day 10**, close the loop:

> Hi [Name] — last note from me, I don't want to clutter your inbox. If ITM
> reporting is something you'd ever want to talk through, I'm at [phone]. Either
> way, thanks and good luck with the season.

Three touches, then stop. In a market this small, a fourth costs more than the
interview is worth.

## Track C — Phone

Better than email for this buyer. Small-shop owners answer their phones and
dislike email.

> "Hi, is this [Name]? My name's [Name], I'm not selling anything — I'm doing
> research on how contractors handle NFPA 25 reporting and I'm trying to talk to
> a dozen or so people who actually deal with it. Is now a bad time?"

*"Is now a bad time?"* invites an honest no and gets you a scheduled call instead
of a rushed one. If they have ten minutes right now, take it — run the first
four questions and ask to finish later.

## Track D — Referral chain

The compounding one, and the highest-yield line in the whole sprint. At the end
of every interview, without exception:

> "This was really useful. Who else should I be talking to? Ideally someone
> whose setup is different from yours — bigger, smaller, different state."

Then, the part most people skip:

> "Would you be willing to introduce me, or is it better if I mention your name?"

An offered intro converts several times better than a name you cold-contact. Ask
for the intro explicitly; most people say yes and simply wouldn't have offered.

"Different from yours" matters — it fights the drift where twelve interviews all
come from one referral cluster and share one operating model, which produces a
consistent-looking result that generalises to nothing.

## Scheduling

Send a calendar link, but also offer to just pick a time — some of these buyers
will not use a booking page and will silently drop out rather than say so.

Confirm the day before by text. No-show rate on cold-sourced calls is real; a
one-line text roughly halves it.

## Weekly targets

| Week | Contacts out | Interviews done | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 120 (A + B) | 4 | 4 |
| 2 | 130 (B + C + referrals) | 8 | 12 |

If week 1 ends under two completed interviews, stop sending and fix the message.
Volume cannot rescue a message that isn't landing, and every name you burn
sending it is one you can't reuse.
