## The one rule

**You are not selling. You are asking for help understanding a workflow.**

Every asset here is built around that, because the moment a prospect believes
you are pitching, three things happen: they become polite, they stop
volunteering problems, and they tell you your idea sounds great. That data is
worse than no data — it produces a confident wrong answer, and Gate 1 exists
precisely to avoid one.

Concretely, this means: no product description, no demo, no "would you use
something that…", no landing page link in the first email. If someone asks what
you are building, the honest answer is *"I don't know yet — that's what these
conversations are for,"* and at this stage it is true.

## The recruiting math

Twelve **completed** interviews, in about two weeks. Run all three tracks at
once; they have very different yields and the cheap ones are slow to start.

| Track | Contacts | → conversations | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Association / warm intro | ~30 | 8–12 | Highest yield. Slowest to open — start day one. |
| Cold outbound | ~200 | 6–10 | ~5–15% reply, roughly half of repliers schedule. |
| Referral chain | grows | +0.5 per interview | The compounding one. Ask every time. |

Budget 250 sourced names to land 12 interviews. If cold reply rate is under 3%,
the problem is the message, not the list — reread
[`02-outreach.md`](02-outreach.md) before sourcing more names.

## Sampling quota — do not skip this

Of the twelve, at least **four owners or principals** and at least **four
operations managers or service managers**.

This is not a diversity nicety. The plan flags a specific Gate 3 failure —
"the pain is real but not priced" — whose most common cause is interviewing the
person who *feels* the pain while a different person *controls the spend*. If
all twelve interviews are with operations managers, Gate 1 passes and Gate 3
fails, and you will not know why. Sampling both roles now is what makes that
diagnosable later.

Also aim for a spread of shop sizes: at least three under 10 technicians, at
least three over 25. Writeup burden per building does not scale linearly with
headcount, and the small shops are where the unbilled-evening-hours pain is
sharpest.

## Practicalities

**Recording.** Ask before you record, every time, on the recording. Several
states require all-party consent and you will be calling across state lines. If
someone declines, take notes instead — a declined recording is not a lost
interview.

**Cold email.** B2B outreach is legal under CAN-SPAM with a real physical
address and a working opt-out. Honour opt-outs immediately; this market is small
and talks to itself, and a reputation for ignoring them is unrecoverable.

**Don't burn the list.** There are only a few thousand of these shops. Sending
200 personalised emails over two weeks is fine; blasting 2,000 templated ones
costs you the market permanently. The list is finite and irreplaceable, which is
exactly why the sequences here are low-volume.
