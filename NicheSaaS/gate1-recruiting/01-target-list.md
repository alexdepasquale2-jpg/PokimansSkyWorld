# Where the buyers are

The buyer is a fire-protection contractor doing NFPA 25 and NFPA 72 inspection,
testing and maintenance work — 5 to 40 technicians, owner-operated or with a
service manager running the ITM side.

Two national bodies, dozens of state chapters, and a handful of events where
several hundred of them are in one room. That is the entire distribution
picture, and it is why this niche scored 5 on reachability.

## National bodies

**AFSA — American Fire Sprinkler Association** ([firesprinkler.org](https://firesprinkler.org/chapters-2/)).
Merit-shop sprinkler contractors. Organised into regions and affiliated state
associations; an affiliated association must hold at least four meetings a year,
which makes chapter meetings a reliable recurring venue rather than an annual
one. Chapter list: [firesprinkler.org/chapterlist](https://firesprinkler.org/chapterlist/).

**NFSA — National Fire Sprinkler Association** ([nfsa.org](https://nfsa.org/)).
Contractors, suppliers, manufacturers, designers, and AHJs. Contractor
membership covers every employee of a member company, so member directories skew
toward whole firms rather than individuals. Chapters:
[member.nfsa.org/chapters](https://member.nfsa.org/chapters).

**AFAA — Automatic Fire Alarm Association** ([afaa.org](https://afaa.org/)).
Founded 1953, the only not-for-profit focused exclusively on fire alarm.
Membership spans installation companies, contractors, central stations,
manufacturers, and municipal fire officials — so it reaches the NFPA 72 half of
the workflow, and its AHJ membership is a way to hear the *rejection* side of a
filing. Active state chapters include New Jersey ([afaanj.org](https://afaanj.org/)),
Illinois ([il-afaa.org](https://il-afaa.org/)), Minnesota
([mnafaa.org](https://mnafaa.org/membership/)), and the Northwest
([nwafaa.com](https://www.nwafaa.com/)).

| **NFPA Conference & Expo** | 14–16 Jun 2027, Henry B. González Convention Center, San Antonio. ~8,000 attendees, ~90 countries | Broad fire/life-safety, not ITM-specific. Lower density of your buyer per attendee |

**For Gate 1, attend — do not exhibit.** The plan budgets one regional event as
an attendee in months 1–3 for exactly this. Walking the floor asking questions
gets you more interviews in a day than a booth does, at a fraction of the cost,
and standing behind a table changes the conversation from "help me understand"
to "what are you selling."

If AFSA45 in late September is inside your Gate 1 window, it is the single
highest-yield two days available — the entire buyer population, self-selected
for caring about the trade.

## Building the list

Work top-down from bodies, not bottom-up from search. Each source below yields
firm names; the contact name comes after.

1. **Chapter member directories.** AFSA and NFSA chapters publish them. Best
   single source: pre-qualified as ITM-doing firms.
2. **State licensing registries.** Most states license fire-protection
   contractors and publish the register. Complete rather than opt-in, so it
   catches non-members — and it usually carries licence class, which tells you
   whether they do ITM or install-only.
3. **AHJ approved-contractor lists.** Some fire marshals publish who may file in
   their jurisdiction. Small but perfectly qualified.
4. **Trade show exhibitor and attendee lists.** Past-year lists are often public.
5. **Job postings.** A shop advertising for an inspector or "ITM technician" is
   a shop with enough ITM volume to have the problem, and the posting names the
   hiring manager often enough to be worth scanning.

### Qualify before you contact

Drop a firm from the list unless it plausibly meets all four:

- Does **recurring ITM**, not just design-build installation
- Between roughly **5 and 40 field technicians**
- Serves a jurisdiction with an active filing requirement
- Is not a division of a national roll-up (procurement is centralised and slow —
  wrong buyer for a pilot)

Ten minutes of qualification per firm beats a hundred extra unqualified names.
Reply rate is a function of fit far more than of volume.
