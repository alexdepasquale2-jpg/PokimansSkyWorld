# The renewal conversation

Month 2. Twenty minutes. Their health signals in front of you.

## Why month 2

A month of runway if the answer is no. And an answer given with time still on
the clock is more honest than one given at cancellation, when people take the
path of least awkwardness and say the product was fine.

## Open with their numbers, not your ask

> "Before I ask you anything — here's what the last eight weeks look like from my
> side. You sent 34 of roughly 40 inspections. Files usually arrive same day now,
> down from two days in week one. Three of your guys are sending, not just you.
> Does that match how it feels from where you sit?"

Two reasons this goes first. It is the strongest case you have and it is made of
their behaviour rather than your claims. And it gives them a chance to correct
you — *"honestly Dave sends them because I keep asking"* is worth more than
anything that follows.

## The ask

> "The pilot rate ends next month. Standard is $[N] a year, and I'd keep the
> onboarding waived since you've already done that work with me. Do you want to
> keep going?"

**Then stop.** Same discipline as Gate 3.

## If yes

Get it signed inside a week. Enthusiasm at month 2 has decayed by month 3.

Then ask the question that is actually worth more than the renewal:

> "What would have made you say no?"

People answer this honestly *after* committing, and almost never before. It is
the cheapest product research available and it is available exactly once per
customer.

Then the referral, per [`../gate1-recruiting/02-outreach.md`](../gate1-recruiting/02-outreach.md):

> "Who else should be using this? Would you introduce me?"

A renewing customer is the strongest referral source you will ever have, and
this is the moment of maximum goodwill.

## If no

**Do not counter.** No discount, no extra month, no feature promise.

This is the discipline that makes the gate mean something — see
[`README.md`](README.md). A save offer converts the logo and destroys the
measurement, and at this stage the measurement is worth more.

Instead:

> "That's genuinely useful to know, and I'd rather you told me now than in a
> month. Can I ask what would have had to be different?"

Then go straight to [`03-churn-postmortem.md`](03-churn-postmortem.md) while it
is fresh.

## If "let me think about it"

The most common answer and the most misread. It usually means no, delivered
politely.

Do not accept the deferral without a date:

> "Of course. Can I call you Thursday? And so I'm not guessing — is there a
> specific thing you're weighing, or is it more that it hasn't obviously earned
> its place yet?"

That fork is the useful part. A specific concern is addressable. "Hasn't
obviously earned its place" is a churn dressed politely, and treating it as a
maybe wastes the month you have left.

## What not to do

**Don't demo new features.** They are deciding whether the thing they have used
for two months is worth paying for. A roadmap pitch says the current product
isn't enough, which is the argument against you.

**Don't invoke sunk cost** — "after all the setup we've done." They will renew
resentfully and churn at month 12, and you will have learned nothing.

**Don't negotiate against yourself.** If they hesitate, wait rather than reaching
for a lower number. Silence has better odds than a discount and it preserves the
signal.

**Don't take a champion's yes as the company's yes.** If your contact isn't the
one who signs, ask who is and get in that room. This is the same
feels-the-pain-versus-controls-the-spend split that
[`../gate1-recruiting/README.md`](../gate1-recruiting/README.md) samples for at
Gate 1 — it reappears here, and at higher stakes.
