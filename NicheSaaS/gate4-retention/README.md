# Gate 4 retention assets

For months 1–6 of the pilots: watching whether they stick, asking for the
renewal, and learning honestly from the ones that don't.

```
01-health-signals.md     weekly leading indicators, and what each one predicts
02-renewal-conversation.md   the month-2 ask
03-churn-postmortem.md   what to extract when one doesn't convert
scorecard.md             the verdict
```

## Gate 4 is a falsification test, not a sales gate

This is the thing that makes these assets different from a normal renewal
playbook, and it is worth being blunt about.

Every earlier gate can be rescued by better execution. Gate 1 fails → better
interviews or a different candidate. Gate 2 fails → the value hypothesis is
wrong at current capability. Gate 3 fails → wrong buyer or wrong artifact, both
fixable.

**Gate 4 failing means the premise was wrong.** The plan's whole thesis is that
this is non-discretionary spend on a legal calendar
([`../README.md`](../README.md)). Mandatory work does not get dropped when the
quarter is tight. So a product serving mandatory work should retain far better
than SaaS norms — and if it doesn't, the buyer found the work skippable, or found
a cheaper route to compliance, and no amount of feature work or discounting
addresses that.

**Target: ≥70% of pilots convert to paid annual.** Below that, the correct
response is to re-examine the niche, not the roadmap.

Which means these assets are built to **detect the truth**, not to maximise
conversion. That is the opposite of how renewal playbooks are usually written,
and the difference shows up in three places:

- **No save offers.** A discount that rescues a renewal destroys the measurement.
  You would convert the logo and lose the finding, which at this stage is the more
  valuable of the two.
- **The post-mortem matters more than the renewal.** Three churns with the same
  reason is worth more than three renewals, because it tells you something you
  can act on.
- **Leading indicators are tracked to predict the verdict, not to trigger
  interventions.** If you intervene every time a signal dips, you are measuring
  your own attentiveness rather than the product's necessity.

## The one intervention that is legitimate

Fixing something broken. If a pilot stops sending files because the output was
wrong twice and they lost confidence, that is a product failure, not a demand
signal — fix it, say so, and note in the scorecard that this account had an
intervention so the number keeps its meaning.

The line: **fix defects, never buy loyalty.**

## Timing

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Weekly, months 1–3 | Log health signals ([`01-health-signals.md`](01-health-signals.md)) |
| Month 2 | Ask for the renewal ([`02-renewal-conversation.md`](02-renewal-conversation.md)) |
| Month 3 | Discount expires; conversion happens or doesn't |
| Within a week of any churn | Post-mortem ([`03-churn-postmortem.md`](03-churn-postmortem.md)) |
| Month 6 | Verdict ([`scorecard.md`](scorecard.md)) |

Asking at month 2 rather than month 3 is deliberate. It leaves a month to fix
whatever the answer surfaces, and a "no" delivered with a month still to run is
far more candid than one delivered at the point of cancellation.
