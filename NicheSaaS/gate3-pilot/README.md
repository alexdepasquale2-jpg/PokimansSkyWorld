# Gate 3 pilot assets

For the conversation that follows Gate 2: you take the five generated reports
back to the interviewee they came from, show them beside the human-written
versions, and ask for money.

```
01-pilot-offer.md      what you're selling, at what price, on what terms
02-walkthrough.md      the script for presenting the five reports
03-objections.md       the predictable objections, answered honestly
pilot-agreement.md     plain-language term sheet
roi-calculator.html    their numbers in, their dollar figure out (offline, no deps)
```

## The pass condition, and why it's harsh

**≥2 signed paid pilots with payment collected.** Not a verbal yes. Not a letter
of intent. Not "invoice me next quarter." Money received.

Every softer signal is abundant and free in this market, including in the
version of this market where the product fails. A collected payment is the first
honest signal the sprint produces, and softening the bar here is how a team
spends the next six months building for people who were being nice.

## What you are actually selling

Not software — at Gate 3 there is no software, only a harness that produced five
reports on a laptop. You are selling **a paid commitment to keep going**, and
what the customer buys is:

- their inspections turned into filed-quality drafts, for a fixed period
- direct access to you while it happens
- influence over what gets built

That is an honest offer and it is enough. Do not imply a product exists, a
timeline you can't hold, or features you haven't built. A pilot sold on a
misdescription converts to a churn at Gate 4 and takes a reference customer with
it.

## Sequencing inside the meeting

1. **Show, don't describe.** Their report, their building, side by side.
2. **Let them find the errors.** They will. That's the point — see
   [`02-walkthrough.md`](02-walkthrough.md).
3. **Their numbers, not yours.** Open the calculator and fill it in from their
   own Gate 1 capture sheet.
4. **Ask for the money.** Specific number, specific term, in one sentence, then
   stop talking.

## The thing most likely to go wrong

You will be tempted to present the reports as finished, because you want the
yes. Don't. Lead with what the system got wrong — the walkthrough script is
built around doing that deliberately.

A compliance buyer who discovers an error you glossed over stops trusting the
whole demo, and reasonably so: their licence signs the filing. A buyer who
watches you point at your own errors first concludes you understand the stakes.
The second conversation is the one that produces a cheque.
