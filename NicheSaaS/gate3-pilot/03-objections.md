# Objections

Answer honestly. This buyer's licence signs the filing, and a salesy answer to a
liability question ends the conversation permanently — they will conclude you
don't understand what they're carrying, and they'll be right.

---

**"How do I know it's accurate enough?"**

> "You don't yet, and neither do I. On your five inspections it recovered [X] of
> [Y] deficiencies and flagged one thing your inspector explicitly didn't call.
> That's why it's a draft with a human signing it, and why I'm asking for three
> months rather than a contract. If review isn't faster than writing, you should
> stop paying me."

Never claim an accuracy number you have not measured on their files.

---

**"Who's liable if it gets something wrong?"**

> "You are, the same as today — your inspector reviews and signs, and nothing
> files without that. I'm not changing where the liability sits and I couldn't
> if I wanted to. What I can do is give the reviewer a diff showing what was
> said, what was extracted, and what the system is unsure about, so the review
> is a real check rather than a rubber stamp."

Do not offer to indemnify. You cannot back it, and offering signals you haven't
thought about it.

---

**"Our AHJ won't accept a computer-generated report."**

> "The AHJ accepts the format they always accept, signed by the person who
> always signs it. Nothing about the filing changes — this affects how the
> document gets written, not who attests to it. If your AHJ has a specific form,
> send it to me and it becomes the output format."

If they're unsure what their AHJ requires, that's an opening: offer to work it
out together during the pilot. It's genuinely useful and it's moat-building
configuration work.

---

**"We already pay for [Inspect Point / BuildingReports / ServiceTrade]."**

> "Keep it. That's your scheduling and dispatch and I'm not replacing it — this
> is the writeup, which is the part you told me your guys do at home. If it
> works you'd run both, and eventually I'd export into yours."

Resist the urge to attack the incumbent. They chose it, and criticising the
choice criticises them.

---

**"My techs won't use it."**

The flip-trigger objection — take it seriously rather than answering past it.

> "That's the thing I'm most worried about, honestly. It's built so there's
> nothing to tap — he talks, he takes photos, that's the whole interaction. But
> if your guys won't narrate into a phone, this doesn't work and I'd rather find
> that out on your five inspections than in a year."

If several interviewees say this with conviction, log it against the flip
trigger in [`../gate1-recruiting/scorecard.md`](../gate1-recruiting/scorecard.md).

---

**"Can we start free and pay if it works?"**

> "I'd rather not, and it's not about the money — at this price it barely covers
> the compute. It's that free pilots don't get files sent, because nobody's
> accountable for making them work. Paying is what makes both of us take the
> three months seriously. If it doesn't work, cancel and we've both learned
> something for a few hundred dollars."

Hold this line. A free pilot converts a paying market into an unpaying one and
tells you nothing about the question the gate is asking.

---

**"Send me a proposal and I'll take it to my partner."**

Real, in a partnership. Don't fight it — shorten it.

> "Happy to. Can I be on that call? Not to sell — the questions your partner
> will have are the technical ones, and I can answer those faster than a
> document can."

Then send [`pilot-agreement.md`](pilot-agreement.md) the same day, with a
specific follow-up date. "I'll circle back" is where pilots go to die.

---

**"Where does your guidance come from? Is that NFPA text?"**

The question the pivot creates. Answer it straight — a compliance buyer who
suspects you are quietly redistributing licensed content will not sign.

> "No, and deliberately not. What you're reading is our own procedure library —
> what to check, what makes it a deficiency, what to photograph. We wrote it. Each
> procedure cites the clause it relates to so your reviewer can turn to your copy
> of the standard, which is the authoritative text. We're not reselling you NFPA;
> you already have NFPA. We're telling you what to do with it."

If they push on accuracy of the mapping: *"If a citation is ever wrong, that's a
bug and I want to hear about it — the system flags any reference it can't find in
our library, and I'd rather show you a gap than a plausible-looking error."*

**Do not oversell this as a feature.** It is the honest position, and framing it
as a competitive advantage to a buyer who has not raised it invites a question
about whether the guidance is authoritative. It isn't — theirs is.

---

**"What happens to my data?"**

> "Your inspections stay yours. I use them to produce your reports and to check
> whether the system's getting better. I won't share them or use them for anyone
> else's account. If you cancel, I'll delete them on request and confirm when
> it's done."

Then actually do that. Write it into the agreement.

---

**"What if you disappear?"**

Fair question to a one-person operation. The honest answer:

> "Then you're out three months of a few hundred dollars and you've still got
> every report I produced, in your format, in your files. I'm not asking you to
> migrate anything or shut anything off. That's deliberate — you should be able
> to walk away cheaply, and at this stage you can."

---

## Two you should not answer

**"Can you also do [scheduling / invoicing / a mobile app]?"** — "Not for a
while, and not during the pilot." Anything else and you have committed a
three-person team to an eighteen-month v1. See the out-of-scope list in
[`../03-product-concept.md`](../03-product-concept.md).

**"When will it be finished?"** — "I don't know yet, and I'd rather say so than
give you a date I'd miss." The pilot is three months of real work, not a
countdown to a launch you cannot schedule.
