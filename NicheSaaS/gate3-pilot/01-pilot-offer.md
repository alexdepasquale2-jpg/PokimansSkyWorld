# The pilot offer

## Shape

| | |
|---|---|
| **Price** | $500–1,500 per month |
| **Term** | 3 months, paid monthly, cancel any time with 30 days' notice |
| **Volume** | Up to 40 inspections/month |
| **Rollout** | Start with **one technician**, expand at month 2 |
| **Onboarding** | Waived for design partners (normally $1,500–3,000) |
| **Commitment from them** | A named contact, inspection files within 48h, 30 min of feedback a week |
| **Commitment from you** | Drafts back within one business day, direct line to you |

### Picking the number

Anchor on their own arithmetic, not on a price list. From the ROI calculator:
land at roughly **10–20% of the annual value** the pilot recovers, divided by 12.

- Shop of 8 techs, ~15 inspections/week → **$500/mo**
- Shop of 20 techs, ~40 inspections/week → **$1,000/mo**
- Shop over 25 techs, or multi-jurisdiction → **$1,500/mo**

Below $500 stops being a qualification filter and starts being a favour people
grant to be polite — which defeats the entire purpose of charging.

### Start with one technician

Not the shop. One volunteer, ideally one who already dictates texts rather than
types them.

Many of these shops have a failed rollout behind them — a tool that was pushed
to the whole field team and quietly died. A team carrying that scar tissue is
not evaluating your product, it is evaluating a repeat of a bad experience, and
a whole-shop rollout to them is the highest-risk possible start.

One technician for a month gives you an internal champion with a real story
instead of a mandate from the office, and it makes the month-2 expansion a
decision the field team makes rather than one made at them. It also caps the
blast radius if the capture flow turns out to need work.

Price the pilot on the shop, not the seat — you are not selling seats yet, and
a per-seat pilot price gives them a reason to keep it at one forever.

### Why paid, when free would convert better

Free converts better and measures nothing. The question Gate 3 asks is not "will
they take it" but "is this priced" — and a free pilot answers a question nobody
asked. It also sets an anchor you spend the next year fighting.

If someone genuinely cannot get a small recurring spend past their own process,
that is a finding about the buyer, not a reason to discount. Note it and move to
the next name.

## What's included

- Inspection audio and photos in, filed-quality draft reports out
- Standards citations on every deficiency
- Deficiency-to-quote conversion using their price book
- Their jurisdiction's output format
- A human review step — **nothing is ever filed automatically**

## What's explicitly not included

Say this out loud in the meeting. Every line here is a promise you would
otherwise be assumed to have made:

- No scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, or accounting
- No mobile app — capture is by phone browser or by handing you the files
- No integration with their existing system yet
- No uptime guarantee
- No claim that their AHJ will accept the format sight-unseen

## Waiving onboarding, not price

The onboarding fee is the qualification filter and the moat-building work
(templates, jurisdictions, price book). Waiving it for design partners is a real
concession that costs you labour and not principle. Discounting the monthly
price instead teaches them the number is soft and makes the Gate 4 renewal
conversation harder than it needs to be.

## Making payment easy

Bring a Stripe payment link to the meeting. "I'll send an invoice" converts
worse than a link they can pay while you're sitting there, and the gate's pass
condition is money *received* — every day between yes and payment is a day the
yes can evaporate.

If they need a PO or a signed document first, that's fine and normal — use
[`pilot-agreement.md`](pilot-agreement.md), agree a date, and diary it.

## Converting to annual (Gate 4)

Raise it at month 2, not month 3. Annual at the standard rate, onboarding still
waived, price locked for 12 months.

Per the plan, **≥70% of pilots must convert to paid annual**. Below that, the
vital-purpose thesis is disproven regardless of how the pilots felt — that
number is a thesis test, not a sales target, so resist the urge to rescue it
with discounts. A discounted renewal tells you nothing about whether the work
was mandatory.
